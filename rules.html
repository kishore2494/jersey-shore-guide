Guide / How it decides

How it decides

The rules the system follows by itself, with the numbers it uses today. Every number is a setting: change it in Settings or Automations.

Estimates

What the customer sees
Sections, each with one price. Materials and labour stay inside the section and are never printed. Optional add-ons are priced apart and are not in the total until the customer takes them.
How long a quote is good for
30 days from the day it is sent; the date is printed on the proposal.
Sales tax
6.625% (New Jersey) on taxable lines. An ST-8 (capital improvement) estimate has no sales tax.
Follow-ups, by themselves
If the customer hasn't answered: an email 2 days after it was sent, another 2 days later, then a call ticket 2 days after that. Only for estimates sent in the last 30 days. Stop them for one estimate if the customer asked for time.
Won
Pressing Won (or the customer accepting online) makes the contract, the payment schedule, the job and its work items in one step. The salesperson's commission and the job costing follow the job from then on.
Lost
Lost asks why (price, went with someone else, timing, no response…). It counts in the close rate, and Reopen brings it back as a draft.

Contracts and payments

Payment schedule
The standard schedule is Deposit 30%, Start of work 60%, Completion 10%. Pick another template on the estimate (pavers, landscape, or your own), set up in Settings.
Signature
Contracts over $1,000 need the customer's signature.
Card surcharge
3.5% is added to card payments and shown on invoices and contracts.
Extras and tracked time
Extras are priced at $95 per man-hour; time-and-materials jobs bill tracked time at the extras rate.
Change orders
Lines and hours, printed for a signature; once approved, a work item for the crew. Billed now or with the final invoice, and shown apart from the contract.

Invoices and what's owed

Invoice total and balance
Every invoice shows its own total, what is paid and what is due; the customer's whole account balance is in its own box, so the two are never mixed up.
Past-due reminders, by themselves
One friendly statement per customer when an invoice is 7 days past due, then every 7 days; after 2 reminders the office gets a collection call ticket. Paying up resets the count. Balances brought over from Service Autopilot are left out until you say otherwise.
Late fee
Off (no late service charge).
Declined card
The customer is asked to update their card, and a call goes on the office list.
Month-end
Customers billed monthly are invoiced in one month-end run: one draft per job, waiting on the Drafts tab to check, then Email all or Print all.
QuickBooks
An export of customers, invoices and payments as files QuickBooks imports, until the direct QuickBooks Online connection is switched on.

Commission and job costing

When commission is earned
When the job is fully paid.
What it is paid on
The job's profit.
Job costing
Contract plus change orders, against labour at your burn rate ($45 per crew-hour today), materials, subcontractors and other costs, live while the job runs. A new burn rate has a start date, so past jobs keep theirs.

Scheduling and the crews

Multi-day jobs
One bar across the days; the weekend rule is set per job.
Too many days
Scheduling warns when a crew would work more than 5 days in a week, for example a Saturday on top of a full week.
Permits
A job waiting on a township permit can't be started until the permit is approved.
Customer notices
The day before, customers get their date for these kinds of work: landscape, maintenance. The crew arrives "early in the morning". Sent again if the date moves.
Start and finish photos
A crew must take a photo to start and to finish a job. Time and place are recorded; a start or finish more than 300 meters from the address is flagged to the office. The customer gets "we've arrived".
Crew time
The crew lead clocks in the whole crew. Yard, loading, travel and unloading time is shared across that day's jobs by itself, so every job carries its fair share.
Overtime and payroll
Overtime after 40 hours a week. Missed clock-outs are flagged; a corrected punch keeps the original. Approved weeks export to ADP Workforce Now.

Maintenance, snow and fleet

Maintenance visits
From each customer's plan (weekly, every two weeks, monthly, spring and fall), visits go on the crew's schedule 21 days ahead, in route order. A visit skipped for rain stays skipped.
Snow
Each property is priced by snowfall or by the hour; crews log visits on the phone and the office bills the storm. Salt is counted in tons and ice melt in bags. Sales tax on snow work is off.
Fleet
Oil and tires are "due soon" within 500 miles, inspections within 30 days, machine service within 25 engine hours. The fleet manager gets the list every Monday, and every truck problem a crew reports from the phone.
Containers
A container moves only when a driver finishes the run that moves it, so the yard list is always what's really out.

Emails and texts

Test mode
Until live email is switched on, every email and text goes to the office's test address, never to a customer, at most 8 a day.
Reminders
Appointment reminders the day before, at 10:00, for: estimate, site visit, service.
Surveys
A satisfaction survey 1 day after a job is complete; a score of 3 or less opens a ticket for the office.
Website requests
A request from the website waits on Home until someone accepts it (customer and ticket made); spam is flagged.
Marketing
Customers who opted out are never included. Campaigns go out at most 200 an hour, so the mailbox isn't blocked.
Switch any of it off
Automations lists every message: switch each on or off, change its words, see what is waiting and what went out, stop or resend one.
